# Gnojo Taxonomy

## Purpose

The Gnojo taxonomy defines how knowledge is organized throughout the platform.

A consistent taxonomy improves discoverability, search, learning progression, reporting, and long-term maintainability.

Every published article must belong to one primary category.

Additional tags may be assigned where appropriate, but categories should remain consistent across the platform.

# Category Rules

Every article should have exactly one primary category.

Choose the category that best represents the main learning objective.

Additional relationships should be represented through tags or related articles rather than assigning multiple categories.

---

# Future Metadata

Each article may also include:

- Difficulty
- Estimated Time
- Operating System
- Vendor
- Technologies
- Commands
- Related Articles
- Learning Objectives
- Prerequisites

---

# Future Categories

New categories should only be added when:

- Multiple articles no longer fit existing categories.
- The technology represents a significant area of IT.
- The category supports long-term growth.

Avoid creating categories for individual products unless substantial content justifies it.

---

# Guiding Principles

The taxonomy should remain:

- Simple
- Predictable
- Scalable
- Consistent
- Easy to navigate

The taxonomy exists to help users find knowledge quickly while supporting future platform growth.

It should evolve deliberately rather than frequently.
